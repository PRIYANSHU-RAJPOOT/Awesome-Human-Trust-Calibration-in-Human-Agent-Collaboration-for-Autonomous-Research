# Awesome Human Trust Calibration in Human–Agent Collaboration for Autonomous Research

A curated collection of research papers, datasets, tools, implementations, and learning resources related to **human trust calibration in human–AI and human–agent collaboration**, with a particular focus on autonomous and agentic AI systems used for research tasks.

This repository explores how humans can develop appropriate levels of trust and reliance when collaborating with autonomous AI agents. It covers trust in automation, over-trust, under-trust, algorithm aversion, algorithm appreciation, explainability, uncertainty communication, source provenance, human oversight, and adaptive autonomy.

## Overview

The rapid development of autonomous and agentic artificial intelligence is changing the nature of human–AI collaboration. Unlike traditional AI systems that primarily provide recommendations or predictions, autonomous agents can perform multi-step activities such as planning tasks, searching for information, retrieving sources, analyzing evidence, generating outputs, and using external tools.

These capabilities create significant opportunities for improving the speed and scale of research. However, they also introduce an important challenge: determining how much humans should trust and rely on autonomous AI agents.

The goal of effective human–agent collaboration is not maximum trust. Instead, the objective is **calibrated trust**, where human reliance corresponds appropriately to the actual capabilities, reliability, limitations, uncertainty, and risks associated with an AI system.

Both over-trust and under-trust can reduce the effectiveness of human–AI collaboration. Over-trust may cause users to accept AI-generated claims, summaries, or citations without sufficient verification. Under-trust may cause users to reject useful AI assistance even when the system performs effectively.

This repository brings together research and resources related to trust calibration, appropriate reliance, automation bias, algorithm aversion, algorithm appreciation, explainability, uncertainty communication, source provenance, and human oversight. A particular emphasis is placed on the emerging context of autonomous research agents and how they can augment human researchers while preserving meaningful human control.

## AI-Assisted Research Paper

### Human Trust Calibration in Human–Agent Collaboration for Autonomous Research

This repository is based on an AI-assisted research paper examining how trust influences human reliance on autonomous research agents.

The paper discusses:

* Trust calibration and appropriate reliance
* Over-trust and automation bias
* Under-trust and algorithm aversion
* Algorithm appreciation
* Transparency and AI explanations
* Uncertainty communication
* Source and evidence provenance
* Human expertise
* Adaptive human oversight
* Research gaps in autonomous research agents

The central argument is that human reliance on AI should be proportional to demonstrated AI competence and should decrease as uncertainty and task risk increase.

## Citation Integrity Audit

The references used in the original AI-assisted research paper were systematically audited for authenticity, metadata accuracy, identifier correctness, and claim-citation support.

The audit examined all 10 references in the paper.

### Audit Results

* **Total references:** 10
* **References deeply audited:** 10
* **Verified references:** 10
* **Wrong metadata:** 0
* **Frankenstein references:** 0
* **Fabricated references:** 0
* **Identifier mismatches:** 0
* **Authenticity score:** 100/100
* **Prediction accuracy:** 100%

Eight references were used for substantive claims in the paper, and all eight supported the claims for which they were cited. Two additional references were genuine but were not cited in the main paper body.

The audit demonstrates an important principle: a citation should not be trusted only because it looks professional or contains a valid DOI. Researchers should verify both whether the source exists and whether it actually supports the claim being made.

## Foundational Research Papers

### Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance

**Authors:** John D. Lee and Katrina A. See
**Year:** 2004
**Journal:** Human Factors

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392)

A foundational study explaining how trust influences reliance on automated systems and why appropriate reliance is more important than simply maximizing trust.

### Trust in Automation: Integrating Empirical Evidence on Factors That Influence Trust

**Authors:** Kevin A. Hoff and Masooda Bashir
**Year:** 2015
**Journal:** Human Factors

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720814547570)

This paper presents a framework for understanding dispositional, situational, and learned trust in automation.

### Trust in Automation: Development of a Scale to Measure Trust

**Authors:** Jian-Yun Jian, Ann M. Bisantz, and Colin G. Drury
**Year:** 2000
**Journal:** International Journal of Cognitive Ergonomics

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327566IJCE0401_04)

This paper develops a scale for measuring human trust in automated systems and is useful for research investigating how trust can be evaluated and calibrated.

### Trust and Reliance on Automation: Effects of Error and Experience

**Authors:** Raja Parasuraman and Victor Riley
**Year:** 1997
**Journal:** Human Factors

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872097778543886)

This foundational paper discusses the role of automation in human decision-making and the problems associated with misuse, disuse, and inappropriate reliance.

### Humans and Automation: Use, Misuse, Disuse, Abuse

**Authors:** Raja Parasuraman and Victor Riley
**Year:** 1997
**Journal:** Human Factors

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872097778543886)

This work provides an important framework for understanding how humans may over-rely on automation, fail to use useful automation, or use automated systems inappropriately.

### Trust, Automation, and Human–Machine Interaction

**Authors:** Raja Parasuraman, Thomas B. Sheridan, and Christopher D. Wickens
**Year:** 2000
**Journal:** IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354)

This paper examines levels of automation and provides a framework for understanding how different degrees of system autonomy influence human roles and oversight.

### Human–AI Interaction and Appropriate Reliance

**Authors:** Thomas B. Sheridan
**Year:** 2002
**Journal:** Human Factors

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1518/0018720024499839)

This work discusses human supervisory control and provides useful concepts for understanding human involvement in increasingly autonomous systems.

## Algorithm Aversion and Algorithm Appreciation

### Algorithm Aversion: People Erroneously Avoid Algorithms After Seeing Them Err

**Authors:** Berkeley J. Dietvorst, Joseph P. Simmons, and Cade Massey
**Year:** 2015
**Journal:** Journal of Experimental Psychology: General

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000033)

Demonstrates how people may become reluctant to rely on algorithms after observing them make mistakes, even when algorithms perform well overall.

### Algorithm Appreciation: People Prefer Algorithmic to Human Judgment

**Authors:** Julia M. Logg, Joshua A. Minson, and Don A. Moore
**Year:** 2019
**Journal:** Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes

[Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2018.12.005)

Shows that people can sometimes prefer algorithmic advice over human advice, demonstrating that reliance can be biased toward automation as well as away from it.

### Key Research Areas

Future research on autonomous research agents should investigate:

* How increasing agent autonomy affects human trust
* Whether source provenance improves trust calibration
* How AI errors influence trust during multi-step tasks
* Whether explanations improve appropriate reliance or simply increase acceptance
* How researcher expertise affects reliance on AI agents
* Whether adaptive human checkpoints reduce over-reliance
* How uncertainty should be communicated during autonomous literature review
* Whether long-term interaction produces calibrated trust or excessive dependence
